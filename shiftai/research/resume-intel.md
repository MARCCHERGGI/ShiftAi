## 2. Recruiter / bar-manager resume guidance

- **Resume Optimizer Pro — bartender resume examples**
  https://resumeoptimizerpro.com/blog/bartender-resume-examples
  - "Bar managers spend roughly 6-8 seconds on each resume, looking for three
    things: volume served (covers per shift, sales per night), specific skills
    (POS systems, signature cocktails, certifications), and tone (does this
    person sound like a working bartender or a tourist?)"
  - "An email like partymike420@gmail.com gets your resume rejected before the
    first bullet is read." "A second page signals inability to prioritize."
  - Bullet formula: "action verb plus specific task plus quantified metric plus
    context or outcome." "Include POS system names (Toast, Aloha, Micros) since
    ATS systems scan for exact tool matches."
  - Quantification examples: "served 180 to 220 covers per Friday and Saturday
    shift at a 90-seat craft cocktail bar"; "55-70 covers per hour on solo
    weekend shifts averaging $2,400 per service"; "held variance under 3
    percent on liquor cost through measured pours."
- **A Bar Above — How to write a great bartender résumé**
  https://abarabove.com/how-to-write-a-great-bartender-resume/
  - Managers read professional experience first; "this whole process usually
    takes less than a minute for each applicant." "Never submit a two-page
    résumé." "Spelling mistakes, formatting errors, and typos are not the first
    impression you want to leave." Hand-delivering shrinks the competition.
- **ResumeBuilder.com bartender examples**
  https://www.resumebuilder.com/resume-examples/bartender/ — corroborates
  five-section structure (summary with metrics, quantified experience, skills
  tailored to venue, certifications, education) and high-volume examples
  ("300+ guests per shift... $12,000 in nightly bar revenue with a
  120-drink-per-hour peak rate"; "under 90-second average preparation time").
- **Userve — Bartending: what hiring managers look for**
  https://blog.userve.com/us/bartending-what-hiring-managers-look-for —
  corroborates communication/service emphasis and certification signaling.

## 3. NYC-specific facts

### Wages / tip credit
- **NYS DOL minimum wage for tipped workers** https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-tipped-workers
  and **TipHaus NY tipping laws 2026** https://www.tiphaus.com/blog/new-york-tipping-laws-explained-minimum-wage-pooling-and-your-rights/
  - NYC minimum wage $17.00/hr as of Jan 1, 2026. Tipped food-service workers:
    $11.35 cash wage + $5.65 tip credit. Tipped service (non-food) employees:
    $14.15 + $2.85.
  - Cross-check against listings: the $11.35 cash wage appears verbatim in
    current CA listings (Oiji Mi, estela, Boucherie, The Mulberry, BLT Prime);
    $17.00 posted at Harta, Crane Club, CUERNO.

### Certifications
- **GetTIPS — ATAP certification in NYC** https://www.gettips.com/blog/atap-certification-how-become-tips-certified-nyc
  and **Always Food Safe — NY ATAP** https://alwaysfoodsafe.com/en/alcohol-certification/new-york/all-new-york
  - "When you're told to get a bartending license in NYC, what they actually
    mean is ATAP certification." Not legally mandatory, but employer-required in
    practice; valid ATAP training can reduce SLA penalties up to 25%. TIPS is an
    ATAP-approved provider.
- **ServingAlcohol — NY food handler guide** https://servingalcohol.com/food-handler-card-new-york/
  and https://servingalcohol.com/new-york-food-safety-laws-food-handler-certification-guide/
  - NYC Food Protection Certificate (DOHMH) legally required for supervisors
    under NYC Health Code; "the New York State food handler certificate is not
    valid for New York City." Pairing food handler + ATAP "covers both major
    compliance requirements that New York food and beverage employers look for."
  - Cross-check: Seirēn and the private high-volume listing name the NYC Food
    Handler's Certificate; Café Chelsea hard-requires TIPS.

### Union hotels
- **HTC (Hotel and Gaming Trades Council)** https://hotelworkers.org/ ·
  https://hotelworkers.org/union-workers/hiring-enforcement-office ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_and_Gaming_Trades_Council
  - ~40,000 members; 300+ hotels under contract ≈ 75% of NYC hotel industry.
    "Most union hotel workers in New York City earn nearly $40 an hour — double
    what most hotel workers make without a union." Job placement via the Hiring
    Enforcement Office: hotelworkers.org/hire or in person at 709 8th Ave.

### POS landscape
- **Host Merchant Services POS market share review**
  https://hostmerchantservices.com/articles/point-of-sale-market-share/ and
  **Payments Dive — Toast vs Clover** https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/toast-clover-battle-for-small-eateries/809108/
  - Toast ≈ 24% share concentrated in restaurants (dominant among independents,
    134k locations end-2024); Square ≈ 28% across small business broadly;
    "traditional providers like NCR (Aloha), Oracle (Micros)... retain much of
    the enterprise"; "Oracle MICROS powers everything from hotel restaurants to
    major stadium concessions"; "only Toast and SpotOn are focused entirely on
    restaurants."
  - Cross-check: Toast named verbatim in the Pubkey listing.
